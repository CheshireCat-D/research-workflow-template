# Technical manuscript excerpt

This is an original synthetic exercise. The requested review concerns mathematical correctness and the stated scope. No claim of new research is made.

## Setting
Let n be a positive integer and let X_1,...,X_n be real square-integrable random variables on one probability space. Each has mean mu and variance sigma^2, where sigma^2 is finite. Assume the random variables are identically distributed.

## Proposition
For M_n=(X_1+...+X_n)/n, we have E[M_n]=mu and Var(M_n)=sigma^2/n.

## Proof
Linearity gives E[M_n]=n^(-1) sum_i E[X_i]=mu. Expanding the variance,
Var(M_n)=n^(-2)[sum_i Var(X_i)+2 sum_(i<j) Cov(X_i,X_j)].
Identical distributions imply Cov(X_i,X_j)=0 whenever i differs from j, so the covariance terms vanish.
The remaining sum is n sigma^2, proving the proposition.

## Scope
The claim is about the mean and variance, rather than an exact sampling distribution or an asymptotic approximation.

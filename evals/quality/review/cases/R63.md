# Technical manuscript excerpt

Original synthetic paired-pen feeding exercise; not a real animal study. The requested review concerns the reported design, calculations and conclusion.

## Design and estimand
Six matched blocks contain two pens each, with ten birds per pen. Within each block, a fair independent random assignment gives feed B to one pen and control to the other. There is no cross-pen interference. Individual records are complete. The endpoint is 28-day gain in grams. Pen means are computed first; the estimand is the equally weighted average of the six block differences, feed B minus control. Bird measurements are within-pen subsamples.

## Data and analysis
The six pen-mean differences are 20, -10, 30, 0, 10 and -20 g. Their mean is 5 g, sample standard deviation is sqrt(350) g, and standard error is sqrt(350/6)=7.637626 g. A reference t interval uses a working model of independent approximately normal block differences, df=5 and critical value 2.5705818356363146. It is [-14.633143,24.633143] g.

For a design-based test of the sharp no-effect null, the statistic is the absolute mean difference. Enumerating the 64 equally likely within-block whole-pen label swaps, including ties, gives 44 statistics at least as extreme as observed. Thus the exact two-sided randomization P value is 44/64=0.6875.

## Result
The estimated B-minus-control difference is +5.0 g. At the prespecified two-sided 0.05 level, the gain difference is not statistically significant. The next design decision will use an explicitly chosen meaningful difference and external variance information.
